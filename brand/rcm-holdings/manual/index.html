Personality & voice

Clear, confident and precise.

We write the way our best people talk to clients: knowledgeable, direct and calm under pressure. Complexity is our job — the client should never feel it.

Boilerplate
RCM Holdings is a global freight forwarding and customs brokerage company. We deliver intelligent logistics solutions that connect markets, simplify complexity and drive international trade forward — managing ocean, air and road freight and customs compliance as one accountable partner.
Voice principles

Clear — not complicated

Lead with the answer. Short sentences, plain words, one idea at a time.

We say“Your shipment clears customs Thursday.”Avoid“Clearance is anticipated pending processing.”

Confident — not arrogant

Speak from expertise and evidence, never from superlatives.

We say“98% of our shipments arrive on schedule.”Avoid“The world’s best logistics provider.”

Precise — not technical for its own sake

Use exact figures, dates and terms — and explain them once.

We say“Under FCA terms, risk transfers at our Newark facility.”AvoidUnexplained acronyms and jargon.

Partner-minded — not transactional

Write with the client, about their goals. Use “we” and “you”.

We say“Here’s how we’ll protect your launch date.”Avoid“Per our terms, delays are not our responsibility.”
Tagline usage

Set “Global trade. Intelligently connected.” in uppercase with wide tracking, as in the logo lockup — or in sentence case within copy. Never alter the wording.

Writing the name

“RCM Holdings” in text; “RCM” after first mention. Never “R.C.M.”, “Rcm” or “RCM holdings”.

The symbol

Connection, direction and precision.

The symbol represents the core elements that drive global trade and logistics forward. Every proportion is fixed — use the master artwork only.

  • Four quadrants — the four points of the compass: markets everywhere, connected through one partner.
  • The cross channel — the routes and borders we move goods through, kept perfectly clear.
  • The open centre — transparency: clients always see where their shipment stands.
  • The forward quadrant — Orange Red, pointing down and onward: momentum and progress.
Construction

A square rotated 45°. The inner diamond is 56% of the outer; the channel is 13% of the half-diagonal. Inner corners carry a 10% radius for approachability; outer points stay sharp for precision.

Design development

Brand symbol exploration.

Five routes explored how a single mark could express connection, direction and precision. “Origin” was selected: it is the most balanced, scales down cleanly to 16 px and holds its meaning in one colour. The other routes are archived here for reference only and must not be used.

01 · Origin — selected

The diamond divided by a clear channel. Balanced, legible, ownable.

02 · Rotation

A pinwheel of quadrants. Dynamic, but read as circular rather than forward.

03 · Compass

An elongated compass point. Strong direction, weak at small sizes.

04 · Linkage

Interlocking chevrons and an arrow. Literal, and too complex to reproduce.

05 · Facet

Asymmetric weights. Interesting, but less stable and less trustworthy.

Compact icon

The symbol, on its own.

The compact icon places the reverse symbol on a Navy rounded square. Use it where the brand is already established or the name appears nearby.

  • App icons, social profile avatars and browser tabs.
  • Corner radius 22.4% of the icon size; symbol 70% of the icon width.
  • Supply the full-bleed square version to platforms that apply their own mask (iOS, Android, LinkedIn).
  • Below 24 px use the favicon artwork: its wider channel stays open at small sizes.

Room to lead.

Keep a minimum clear space of X on every side of the logo, where X equals the cap height of the RCM wordmark. No text, image or edge may enter this zone. More space is always better.

Logo misuse

Protect the mark.

Consistency builds recognition and trust. Always use the supplied master files and never modify them. These examples show common errors to avoid.

Don’t stretch, squash or distort the logo.

Don’t rotate or set the logo at an angle.

Don’t recolour the logo outside the brand palette.

Don’t move the Orange Red quadrant.

Don’t add shadows, gradients, bevels or glows.

Don’t rearrange or resize the elements.

Don’t retype the wordmark in another font.

Don’t place the logo on low-contrast backgrounds.

Accessible combinations

Legible for everyone.

All text must meet WCAG 2.2 contrast requirements: 4.5:1 for body text (AA) and 3:1 for large text of 18.5 px bold / 24 px regular and above.

  • Navy, Graphite and White pass AAA in every pairing — use them for all reading text.
  • Orange Red on White passes for large text and graphics only. Use it for headline numerals, rules and icons — not for paragraphs.
  • Orange Red on Navy passes AA — the strongest way to use the accent in text.

Typographic hierarchy

Structured, never shouted.

  • Headlines in Bold, sentence case, tight leading. A closing full stop is part of the voice.
  • Follow major headlines with the Orange Red accent bar.
  • Eyebrows and labels: uppercase, SemiBold, tracked +18%.
  • Align left. Never justify body text.
  • Body text in Graphite; headlines in Navy.

Graphic devices

Built from the symbol.

Four simple devices, each derived from the symbol, give layouts movement and structure. Use one or two per layout — never all four.

The direction line

A single Orange Red hairline rising from lower left to upper right — progress. 1–4 pt, never more than one per layout.

The diagonal plane

A Navy triangle anchored to a corner, often paired with the direction line. It frames content without boxing it in.

Headline
statement.

The accent bar

A short Orange Red bar under headlines and key figures. Width 3–4× its height, aligned left with the text.

The diamond lattice

A quiet grid of diamonds and channels for backgrounds. Keep it under 10% opacity; never behind body text.

Imagery

Five visual themes.

Imagery is cool, deep and precise, lit by warm accents that echo Orange Red. The supplied illustrations set the tone; photography should follow the same direction.

Global connections
Direction & movement
Precision & structure
Intelligent logistics
Speed & efficiency
Photography — do
  • Night aerials of ports, cities and trade routes with light trails.
  • Real operations: terminals, cargo, people at work.
  • Clean architecture with strong diagonal lines.
Treatment
  • Cool blue grade, deep shadows, warm orange highlights.
  • Leave negative space for headlines and the logo.
  • Reverse logo on dark imagery only.
Avoid
  • Clichés: handshakes, globes held in hands, staged smiles.
  • Over-saturated colour, heavy filters, busy collages.
  • Images of competitors’ branded vessels or equipment.

Shipping documentation

Precision on paper.

Commercial invoices, packing lists, quotations and shipping instructions share one system so clients and authorities can find every field instantly.

  • Horizontal logo top-left; document title top-right in Navy Bold caps.
  • Field labels in tracked caps, Graphite 75%; values in Navy.
  • Table headers on Navy; zebra rows in Paper (#F7F7F8).
  • Orange Red only for the total and the header rule — never for data.
  • Fully monochrome-safe: every element remains legible when faxed or printed in black.
Template

templates/documents/rcm-commercial-invoice.html — edit in any code editor and print to PDF (US Letter; switch to A4 in one line).
