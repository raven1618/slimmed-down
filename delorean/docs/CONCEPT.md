# De Lorean: Concept & Brand Brief

**Draft v0.1, for discussion.** Nothing visual gets built until the decisions in section 9 are made.

## 1. The idea in one paragraph

De Lorean is a machine you build to run the parts of your business you never have time for: content, marketing, sales, community, bookkeeping, legal prep and business advice. Inside the machine is a team of AI agents that know your business, watch what's happening and come to you with the work already done: *"Here's what I'd do, here's why. Approve?"* You don't learn prompts or set up tools. You build and tune the machine, and you watch it work as a retro-future city made of your business's data.

## 2. Brand

**Name.** De Lorean (inherited). "DeLorean" is also a registered trademark in the automotive world, so confirm the rights for this use before a public launch.

**Big idea: *Your business, running ahead.*** The machine is always a step ahead: it notices first, prepares first and gets you to the future you want sooner. This keeps the forward-in-time feeling of the name without leaning on any film.

**Promise to the owner.** You make the decisions. The machine does the work and shows its reasoning. It never ships anything without your OK until you've told it that it can.

**Personality.** A brilliant chief operator who loves your business: capable, warm, plainspoken and a little playful. It's confident in its proposals and honest about uncertainty. It's never smug and never uses jargon.

**Voice in practice**

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Saturday sales dipped three weeks running. I drafted a rainy-day brunch offer. Want it live by Friday?" | "Leverage AI-driven insights to optimize your weekend revenue funnel." |
| "I'm not sure about this one. Your last two discount posts underperformed." | "Great idea!!! 🚀" |
| "Needs you: 2 decisions, about 3 minutes." | "You have 47 notifications." |

**Tagline options**
- Your business, running ahead.
- Build it once. Tune it forever.
- The machine behind your business.
- Less admin. More ahead.

## 3. Core metaphor: the machine is a city

You **build** a machine. When you look inside it, you see a **city** made of the data moving through your business right now.

- **From far away it reads as a machine.** You see one object with an engine at its center and gauges around it, humming at a certain speed. You can tell at a glance whether it's running well.
- **Zoom in and it's a city.** Each district is one department (content, sales, money…). Work moves along the streets as light, and buildings grow from real numbers.
- **Zoom in further and you're at street level.** Individual pieces of work (a post, a lead, an invoice, a review) are tangible objects you can pick up, read, approve or send back.

That three-level zoom is the core interface: one world, three levels.

| Zoom | It looks like | What you do there |
|---|---|---|
| Machine | One object, glowing engine, gauges | Check health at a glance, read the briefing |
| District | A neighbourhood for one department | See what the crew is doing, tune it |
| Street | Individual pieces of work | Read, approve, edit, send back |

- **Build** means installing modules (districts). The starting set fits your type of business, and more unlock as you go.
- **Tune** means using dials and switches instead of prompts: voice (playful ↔ formal), autonomy (propose → draft → run), cadence, budget and risk. Tuning is how a non-technical owner trains the AI.
- **The engine** at the center holds the AI's context, meaning everything it knows about your business. It visibly grows as it learns, and every district connects to it with lines of light, a nod to how AI "attention" works.

## 4. Three looks for the data city

All three are retro-future updated for the AI era. Pick one, mix two, or reject all three.

### A. Space-Age Analog: "the warm machine" (my recommendation)
This is the 1960s–70s picture of the future: rounded forms, cream and burnt orange, teal glass, brushed chrome and big friendly dials.
- Buildings look like mainframe cabinets and radio consoles.
- Data travels through glass tubes as glowing capsules.
- Tape reels spin when a crew is working, and towers of punch cards stack up as work ships.
- The engine is a glass dome with a soft light inside that pulses as it thinks.

- **AI update:** a living nervous system of light connects everything. It's an old machine with a new mind.
- **Why:** it's tactile, warm and friendly for a baker or a freelancer, and turning physical dials makes "tuning" feel natural. It also stands out from the sea of neon tech products.
- **Risk:** it can feel nostalgic or slow if the motion isn't crisp.

### B. Vector Metropolis: "the night drive"
This is the 1980s picture: a black horizon, a glowing wireframe grid, a gradient sunset and neon edges.
- Buildings are vector outlines whose height is a live metric.
- Traffic made of light is the data flow.
- The sky shows the forecast.

- **AI update:** an aurora in the sky made of your customers' conversations, with sentiment shown as color.
- **Why:** it reads instantly as retro-future, it's dramatic, and it's great in screenshots and videos.
- **Risk:** it's the most overused retro-future look (synthwave). It can feel cold and "gamer", and it's harder to make cozy.

### C. Circuit Garden: "the living board"
The city sits on a giant circuit board that is slowly being overgrown by light, like a garden.
- Buildings are chips and components, and streets are copper traces with data pulsing along them.
- Zoom out and it's literally a machine you assembled; modules click into sockets.

- **AI update:** neural pathways grow between components as the AI learns, like roots.
- **Why:** it's the most literal "machine you build". Slotting modules into a board is intuitive, and the growing pathways show learning beautifully.
- **Risk:** it looks very techy at first glance, so it needs warm color and soft materials to stay friendly.

**My recommendation:** use A as the base and borrow two things from C: modules that click into sockets (for building) and pathways that grow (for learning). A is the most approachable for all three user types, and the most "machine you tune".

## 5. Feeling intelligent: proactive, contextual, guiding

This is the heart of the product. A machine that feels smart does these eight things:

1. **It already knows you on day one.** Onboarding is a conversation, not a form. You point it at your website, Instagram or Google profile and answer a few questions. It reads everything, builds the context itself and plays it back: "Here's what I understood. Correct me."
2. **It speaks first.** Each day it gives a briefing: what happened, what it prepared and what needs you. That's at most three decisions, each with a time estimate.
3. **Every proposal comes with a reason and a finished draft.** "Because X, I suggest Y. Here it is. Approve?" It brings finished work, not just ideas.
4. **It notices signals.** It watches your calendar, holidays, weather, local events, trends, spikes in comments, slow days, unpaid invoices and low cash. Each signal becomes a proposal.
5. **It learns from every decision.** Approvals, edits and rejections teach it your taste, and it tells you what it learned: "Noted: no exclamation marks, more photos of the team."
6. **It guides one step at a time.** There's always one clear next best move. It asks the one question that matters, not ten.
7. **It earns autonomy.** It starts by proposing. As you approve more, it asks permission to handle routine things itself, and you can always dial it back.
8. **It's honest.** It shows uncertainty, flags legal, financial and tax limits, and never invents facts.

**How the world shows the thinking:**
- When the AI notices a signal, a light travels from the edge of the city into the engine.
- When it has an idea, a capsule forms and travels to the district that will do the work.
- Finished proposals arrive at your dock at the front of the machine.

## 6. Three users, one machine

The machine configures itself to the type of business. It's the same engine, with different starting modules and priorities.

| | Local business (e.g. a bakery) | Solo creator | Freelancer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main goal | Foot traffic, regulars, orders | Audience growth, brand deals | Steady clients, getting paid |
| Starting modules | Content, Community & Reviews, Local Promotions, Money | Content, Audience, Partnerships, Money | Pipeline, Proposals, Invoices, Content |
| Signals it watches | Weather, holidays, slow days, reviews | Trends, posting rhythm, comments, DMs | Leads going cold, unpaid invoices, quiet months |
| Typical proposal | "Rain on Saturday: push the brunch delivery boxes?" | "This topic is rising. Here's a script in your style." | "Two leads haven't replied in 6 days. Follow-ups are drafted." |

## 7. The loop

**Build → Tune → Run → Read → Evolve**

- **Build:** add modules. Each one is an AI crew with its own tools.
- **Tune:** set the dials for voice, autonomy, cadence and budget.
- **Run:** proposals arrive, and you approve, edit or send them back. Approved work ships.
- **Read:** once your accounts are connected, the city reflects real results as traffic, light and power.
- **Evolve:** real results upgrade the machine with new modules, better parts and visual upgrades. Rewards come only from real outcomes, never from busywork: no guilt-trip streaks and no fake urgency.

## 8. Look, sound and motion (for direction A)

- **Palette:** cream `#F4EDE1`, burnt orange `#E8622C`, teal glass `#2BA6A0`, mustard `#E9B44C`, graphite `#3A3F47`. Night mode uses deep navy with amber light.
- **Type:** a wide geometric display face for headings, which gives the space-age feel, and a clean monospace for data and numbers.
- **Shapes:** capsules, rounded rectangles, circles and dials, with thick bevels and soft glass.
- **Motion:** mechanical but smooth. Things click, slide and lock into place, gauges sweep, and tapes spin up when work starts.
- **Sound:** soft relay clicks, a warm tube hum and a satisfying *chunk* when work ships. Sound starts muted.
- **Avoid:**
  - references to any specific film: no time-travel props, clock towers, catchphrases, or the car's silhouette as a logo
  - neon overload
  - crypto-dashboard vibes
  - manipulative gamification

## 9. Decisions needed

1. **Visual direction:** A, B, C or a mix?
2. **Big idea and tagline:** do any of these feel right?
3. **The AI's presence:** should the machine have a named operator character with its own personality, or should the machine itself speak as "De Lorean"?
4. **Hero user for the first prototype:** the bakery, the creator or the freelancer? The machine supports all three, but the first demo should tell one story well.

## 10. What happens next

Once these are decided, the chosen direction becomes a quick visual prototype of the machine-city: the zoom levels, one working district and the daily briefing. That way the idea can be felt before more gets built. The placeholder names in the code will then be replaced with the names decided here.
